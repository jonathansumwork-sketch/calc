# Supabase and Netlify win Label Concepts' portal

**Build a thin custom portal on Supabase Pro (Sydney) for database, login and artwork storage, keep the front end on Netlify Pro with functions pinned to Sydney, and decide the Quote Print link only after Printec answers three questions about Job Import.** The recommended stack costs about **US$45 per month (roughly A$64) at 50 clients and 50 GB of artwork, rising to about US$61-76 (A$87-108) at 500 GB**, excluding email, domain, monitoring and developer time. A leaner variant, Cloudflare Workers ($5) in place of Netlify, saves about US$15 a month but gives up the existing demo and a simpler Sydney-pinning story. Free tiers are not viable for production: Supabase Free pauses after a week of inactivity and has no backups, and Vercel's free plan is explicitly non-commercial. Ready-made web-to-print costs far more than building at this scale, with the best-documented listings starting at US$350-499 per month for Pressero or a roughly US$9,000 one-off licence for PrintQ, and none is confirmed to feed Quote Print. Almost everything is unverified at the integration end: no public documentation of Job Import's file format, price, or whether "Pressero Connect" exists was found, and several price figures below come from summarising tools or third-party listings. A developer or agency is needed under every option, so developer time, not hosting, is the real budget line.

## Pricing is flat and small, so pick on region, residency and lock-in

At 200-2,000 orders a month the traffic is trivial, so the base plan dominates every bill. The cheapest hosting entry is **Cloudflare Workers Paid at $5 per month**, which includes 10M requests, free static assets and no egress fees ([Cloudflare Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)). Railway Hobby is also $5 ([Railway pricing](https://railway.com/pricing)). Netlify Pro is a flat **$20 with unlimited members** and 3,000 credits; the notes' arithmetic puts 20 GB of bandwidth at about 400 credits and 300K requests at about 60, so usage stays inside the plan ([Netlify pricing](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/); [credit docs](https://docs.netlify.com/manage/accounts-and-billing/billing/billing-for-credit-based-plans/credit-based-pricing-plans/)). Vercel Pro is $20 plus **$20 per developer seat**, so a two-developer team pays $40 ([Vercel pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing)). Render Pro became a flat $25 in April 2026, but instance prices could not be verified and a third-party blog suggests about $7 per service ([Render changelog](https://render.com/changelog/updated-plans-for-render-workspaces); [DevCloud blog](https://deploycloud-shopify.devcloudsoftware.com/blog/render-pricing)). Fly.io has no free tier; a 512 MB machine is $3.69 per month with Oceania egress at $0.04/GB ([Fly pricing](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing)). Firebase and Amplify are pay-as-you-go with no platform fee and could run near zero at this scale ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing); [Amplify pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/amplify/pricing/)).

Region is the sharper filter. **Render and Railway have no Australian region** (Singapore is the nearest) and are out if Australian residency matters ([Render regions](https://render.com/docs/regions); [Railway regions](https://docs.railway.com/reference/regions)). Vercel offers syd1 but functions default to Washington DC unless changed ([Vercel regions](https://vercel.com/docs/regions)). Netlify allows a Sydney functions region and defaults to US East ([Netlify functions docs](https://docs.netlify.com/build/functions/optional-configuration/)). Fly has Sydney only, Firebase has Sydney and Melbourne, and Amplify lists Sydney as an endpoint, though SSR support in Sydney was not confirmed ([Fly regions](https://docs.fly.io/reference/regions); [Firebase locations](https://firebase.google.com/docs/functions/locations); [Amplify endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/amplify.html)). Cloudflare runs at a global edge, and R2 storage cannot be guaranteed Australian: location hints such as Oceania are best effort and Australia is not a jurisdiction ([Cloudflare R2 data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/)).

On the backend side, **Supabase Pro at US$25 is the only option that bundles Postgres, auth, 100 GB of files and 250 GB of egress in a Sydney region (ap-southeast-2) without pausing** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); [Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). Because it is plain Postgres, per-customer data isolation by row-level security and customer-specific price tables fit naturally and exit is a standard dump, though the notes mark the RLS and CSV tooling claims as uncited general knowledge to verify. Firebase's NoSQL model is a poorer fit for price lists and reporting, its free Storage quota applies only to US buckets, and at 500 GB with equal egress the notes estimate about $73 per month for storage alone versus about $17-31 on Supabase ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing)). Appwrite Pro (about $25) is comparable but its Sydney availability could not be confirmed. Neon is database-only, and its object storage is not available in Sydney ([Neon regions](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions)). PocketBase is free software but the heaviest to run: it is self-hosted, pre-1.0 and "not recommended for production critical applications yet" ([PocketBase docs](https://pocketbase.io/docs/)). Auth is not a cost driver anywhere, since 500 clients sit far below every free threshold (Supabase 50,000 MAU on free and 100,000 on Pro; Cognito 10,000; Clerk 50,000).

## Estimated monthly cost: roughly A$65 to A$110 for the recommended stack

The table uses the notes' conversion of 1 USD = A$1.4217 and excludes GST, email, domain, monitoring, payment fees and developer time. Artwork lines are my recomputation from list prices, assuming egress equal to stored volume each month; real egress is unknown.

| Scenario | Hosting (Netlify Pro) | Supabase Pro | Extra storage and egress | Total USD | Total AUD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 clients, ~200 orders, 50 GB | $20 | $25 | $0 | ~$45 | ~A$64 |
| ~200 clients, ~800 orders, 200 GB | $20 | $25 | ~$2 | ~$47 | ~A$67 |
| 500 clients, ~2,000 orders, 500 GB | $20 | $25 | ~$16-31 | ~$61-76 | ~A$87-108 |

The 500 GB storage overage is 400 GB at $0.0213, about $8.52; egress of 500 GB against 250 GB included costs $7.50 if cached at $0.03/GB or $22.50 if uncached at $0.09/GB ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). The notes' own scenario table quotes $33.5-50 for Supabase alone at 500 GB, which omits part of this arithmetic, so I use the higher recomputed figures. The lean variant swaps Netlify for Cloudflare Workers at $5, trimming about $15. Storing artwork in Cloudflare R2 would cost only about $0.60, $2.85 and $7.35 at 50, 200 and 500 GB with free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), and Backblaze B2 less ([Backblaze pricing](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)), but the saving is small against the residency story, so keep primary artwork in Supabase Sydney and use R2 or B2 only as an off-site copy, because **Supabase backups cover database metadata, not stored files** ([Supabase backups docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)).

Three cost cautions apply. Supabase's Pro $10 compute credit covers only a Micro instance; Small compute pricing was not retrieved, so budget headroom of perhaps US$15-20 (my guess, unverified). Daily backups for seven days come with Pro, whereas point-in-time recovery is about $100 per month extra and needs at least Small compute, which is not justified for this portal. And Pro's spend cap is on by default and could block uploads if breached, so monitor it or switch it off ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Malware scanning of uploads, a sensible control, would add roughly low tens of dollars if run as an always-on ClamAV container; that figure is an unverified estimate.

## Residency is a customer expectation, not a legal requirement for a small firm

The Privacy Act generally does not cover businesses with turnover of $3M or less, and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme follows Privacy Act coverage, with exceptions such as Commonwealth contracted service providers and businesses trading in personal information ([OAIC small business guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business); [OAIC NDB scheme](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/notifiable-data-breaches/about-the-notifiable-data-breaches-scheme)). The 2024 reforms did not remove that exemption, and removal remains proposed per secondary sources ([APO Bills Digest](https://apo.org.au/sites/default/files/resource-files/2024-09/apo-nid328549.pdf); [Scalesuite](https://www.scalesuite.com.au/resources/privacy-act-changes-for-smes)). One vendor claims full removal from 1 July 2026; that conflicts with the other sources and looks inaccurate ([Cybercraft](https://www.cybercraft.net/privacy-act-compliance)). Even for covered entities, APP 8 regulates overseas disclosure rather than banning it ([OAIC APP Guidelines Ch 8](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0036/256959/APP-Guidelines-Chapter-8-Cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information-October-2025-v1.3.PDF)). Whether Label Concepts is under or over $3M turnover, and whether to opt in voluntarily, is a question for a lawyer; the notes recommend opt-in as cheap insurance.

The practical driver is procurement: council, health and transport customers often ask where data lives, and Sydney is the low-friction answer. That argues for Supabase Sydney plus functions pinned to Sydney, and for accurate disclosure that email, error monitoring and CDN logs may process data offshore. Supabase confirms SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001, but the report and certificate are limited to the Team plan (US$599) and above ([Supabase security](https://supabase.com/security)), so Label Concepts would inherit the claim without being able to hand over the report on Pro. Basic controls cost little: email verification, optional customer MFA and mandatory staff MFA (Supabase Auth supports TOTP, per the [MFA docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-mfa)), private buckets with short-lived signed URLs, file-type and size limits, and a quarantine-then-scan pattern ([Google Cloud architecture](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/automate-malware-scanning-for-documents-uploaded-to-cloud-storage)).

## Buying a platform costs 5 to 10 times more and may not reach Quote Print

Public pricing for ready-made web-to-print is thin and mostly third-party. Pressero is listed at US$499 per month on Capterra and US$350 on GetApp, neither confirmed by the vendor ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/158641/Pressero/); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/industries-software/a/pressero/)). At the notes' conversion, that is roughly A$500-710 a month against about A$64-108 for the build, before any connector fee. PrintQ is listed near US$9,000 one-time and over US$10,000 in one review, from only five reviewers ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/ca/web-to-print/printq-profile/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/181116/Magento/)). DesignNBuy shows a US$1,490-1,990 setup plus about US$150-290 monthly on its own pages, but that page is internally inconsistent and Capterra shows US$499 ([DesignNBuy pricing](https://designnbuy.com/web-to-print-plans-pricing/)). OnPrintShop and PrintXpand are quote-only, and PrintXpand reviewers warn of heavy custom work and extra charges ([Spendbase](https://www.spendbase.co/?p=34647); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/industries-software/a/printxpand/)). ShopVOX, a US sign-shop system with a customer portal, lists US$99 per month, but reviewers describe a steep learning curve and hard pricing setup, and it is US-centred ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/155218/shopVOX/); [GetApp reviews](https://www.getapp.co.uk/reviews/110507/shopvox)). A WooCommerce measurement-pricing plugin is about US$59 and handles square-metre pricing ([WooCommerce](https://woocommerce.com/products/measurement-based-pricing/)), but customer-specific price lists, approvals and ERP export would need custom code, making it a build in disguise. None of these figures is AUD, none confirms Australian hosting, and the vendors' real prices need quotes.

Buying wins only if a vendor ships a supported Quote Print connector. Evidence for one is mixed. QuotePrint's own site lists "Pressero Connect" and "XMPIE Connect" among its optional modules, alongside "Job Import" and "Job Export" ([QuotePrint Our Solutions](https://quoteprint.com.au/our-solutions/)). Yet the Quote & Print cloud home page showed none of those module names, and no function descriptions, formats or prices exist anywhere ([Quote & Print](https://www.quoteandprint.com.au)). The one documented precedent is Worldwide Printing installing XMPie integrated into Quote & Print to remove double entry ([Print21](https://www.print21.com.au/news/worldwide-printing-installs-xmpie-web-to-print)), which is an enterprise franchise network, not a small signage shop. The catalogue-oriented modules also look aimed at offset and digital print rather than width-by-height signage pricing; that is my inference from module names, not documentation. So a custom portal that computes area-based quotes and hands a job to Quote Print is the likelier fit, with the integration being the main risk.

## Questions for Printec and Quote Print decide the integration design

The notes could not establish how orders get into Quote Print, so the build's integration layer is provisional. If Job Import accepts a file, the realistic pattern is that the portal writes one file per order to an SFTP or shared folder, staff load it as a draft quote or job, and a Job Export file returns the job number. Mitigations are to treat the portal as the order source of truth, use idempotent file names carrying the portal order ID, run a daily reconciliation, and show portal quotes as indicative until staff confirm. This is engineering judgement, not documented Quote Print behaviour ([QuotePrint notes, file-based pattern](https://quoteprint.com.au/our-solutions/)).

The unknowns to resolve, in priority order, are these. First, the Job Import specification: file format (CSV, XML or other), whether it creates quotes or jobs, field mapping for custom dimensions, material and finishing, how artwork files are referenced, and whether it is included in the current licence. Second, whether Job Export or any web service can return job status and numbers, and whether Printec offers custom programming and at what cost. Third, how Quote Print's estimating rules compare with portal pricing, since mismatches create quote disputes. Fourth, whether "Pressero Connect" and "XMPie Connect" exist, what they cost, and which web-to-print systems they accept; this decides whether buying is even viable. Fifth, the relationship between QuotePrint (quoteprint.com.au) and Quote & Print (quoteandprint.com.au): shared 1981 origins, distributors and sector lists suggest one lineage, but no page says so, and the Quote & Print cloud on Azure/SQL may differ from the desktop product Printec supports. Printec Solutions is the Victoria and Tasmania partner ([QuotePrint support network](https://quoteprint.com.au/our-national-support-network/)), listed at +61 3 5967 2488, and no website or press coverage for it was found. The notes also found no link between Worldwide's OPM platform and QuotePrint; do not assume OPM is an option.

## Where the notes conflict or are unverified

Several figures should be rechecked before committing. The exchange rate differs between notes: the backend notes use 1 USD = A$1.4217 from the Frankfurter API dated 21 Oct 2026 ([Frankfurter](https://api.frankfurter.dev/v1/latest?from=USD&to=AUD)), while the hosting and web-to-print notes assume an unsourced 1.50; I use 1.4217 throughout, so hosting-note AUD figures run about 5% high. Appwrite Pro is listed at $25 with 2 TB bandwidth in a September 2025 changelog but $15 with 300 GB on older pages ([Appwrite changelog](https://appwrite.io/changelog/entry/2025-09-01.md)). Amplify's free tier reads as 12 months in one place and 6 months in another ([Amplify pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/amplify/pricing/)). Netlify Sydney functions need Pro per the hosting notes, while the privacy notes say older forum posts suggesting that may be outdated; the recommendation buys Pro anyway. Cloudflare's Melbourne presence rests on a 2019-era press item and a third-party status tracker. Sydney prices for S3 and Google Cloud Storage did not render, so those rows are estimates. Real traffic, egress and file sizes are assumptions, and commercial-use terms for most free tiers were not checked, though Vercel Hobby is explicitly non-commercial ([Vercel Hobby docs](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)). Usability for non-developers rests on feature facts rather than reviews, and no independent total-cost studies were found.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which cloud" to "who builds and maintains it". Platform differences at this scale amount to under A$50 a month, whereas web-to-print subscriptions, a US$9,000 licence, or an agency's integration work are one to two orders of magnitude larger, and no option removes the need for a developer. The decision therefore turns on Printec: if Job Import is a simple documented file drop, the Supabase and Netlify build is clearly cheapest and best matched to variable-size quoting; if only a vendor-built connector exists, the build still wins unless that connector is cheap and supports a specific portal product.

A sensible sequence is to ask Printec the questions above before any further build spend, keep the Netlify demo as the front-end prototype while moving data to a paid Supabase Sydney project, and get a security-questionnaire sample from one target council or healthcare customer to confirm what residency evidence is really required. Only then price a one-off developer engagement for auth, quote logic, CSV imports and the Job Import writer.
